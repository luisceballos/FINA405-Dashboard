FX Cross-Rate Arbitrage Quiz

Practice computing cross rates, detecting mispricing, and calculating arbitrage profits using USD, AUD, and CAD.

📋 5 Questions
🏆 5 Points Total (1 pt each)
💱 Currencies: USD · AUD · CAD
👤 Instructor: Luis Ceballos, PhD
📊 Given Market Quotes — Use These for All Questions
AUD/USD 0.6450
CAD/USD 0.7320
AUD/CAD (market) 0.8810

How to read: AUD/USD = 0.6450 means 1 AUD = 0.6450 USD  |  CAD/USD = 0.7320 means 1 CAD = 0.7320 USD  |  The AUD/CAD market quote (0.8810) is what a FX broker quotes. Your job: compute what it should be.

1
Step 1 — Identify the Common Currency
1 pt

You want to compute the cross rate AUD/CAD (how many CAD does 1 AUD buy?). You have two direct quotes against USD: AUD/USD = 0.6450 and CAD/USD = 0.7320.

What is the common (shared) currency that lets you form the cross rate by dividing?

Hint: Both AUD/USD and CAD/USD express their value in USD. Dividing one by the other cancels USD and leaves AUD vs CAD. Formula: AUD/CAD = (AUD/USD) ÷ (CAD/USD)
2
Step 2 — Compute the No-Arb Cross Rate
1 pt

Using the division method, calculate the no-arbitrage AUD/CAD cross rate.

Formula
AUD/CAD = AUD/USD ÷ CAD/USD = 0.6450 ÷ 0.7320 = ?
Computed AUD/CAD = CAD per AUD
Hint: Divide 0.6450 by 0.7320. The result tells you how many CAD you get for 1 AUD if you go through USD. Round to 4 decimal places.
3
Step 3 — Interpret the Cross Rate
1 pt

Your computed no-arb AUD/CAD rate is ≈ 0.8811. The FX broker quotes AUD/CAD at 0.8810 in the market.

Which statement is correct?

Hint: Compare 0.8811 (computed) vs 0.8810 (market). Which is larger? If the computed rate > market rate, the market is underpricing AUD in CAD terms — a positive (but tiny) mispricing.
4
Detect Mispricing — Is There an Arbitrage Opportunity?
1 pt

Suppose the broker now quotes AUD/CAD at 0.8760 instead. Your computed no-arb rate remains 0.8811.

Calculate the mispricing in pips and determine whether an arbitrage opportunity exists (assume typical AUD/CAD bid-ask spread = 4 pips).

Computed Rate
0.8811
≠
Market Quote
0.8760
Formula — Pips
Pips = |Computed − Market| × 10,000   →   |0.8811 − 0.8760| × 10,000 = ?
Mispricing = pips
Based on your pip count and the 4-pip spread, is this profitable?
Hint: 0.8811 − 0.8760 = 0.0051 → multiply by 10,000 → 51 pips. Compare to the 4-pip spread. If the gap > spread, the arbitrage profit net of transaction costs is positive.
5
Profit Calculation — $100,000 USD Investment
1 pt

You identify the mispricing from Q4 (market AUD/CAD = 0.8760 vs no-arb = 0.8811). You invest $100,000 USD executing a triangular arbitrage:

USD → AUD → CAD → USD, exploiting the mispriced AUD/CAD rate.

Step A — Convert USD to AUD
AUD/USD = 0.6450 → divide USD by the rate:
$100,000 ÷ 0.6450 = ? AUD
Step A result = AUD
Step B — Convert AUD to CAD (use market rate 0.8760)
AUD/CAD (market) = 0.8760 → you're selling AUD for CAD:
AUD from Step A × 0.8760 ÷ 1 = ? CAD
Wait — AUD/CAD = CAD per AUD, so multiply AUD × 0.8760 gives you CAD? No: 1 AUD = 0.8760 CAD means you get 0.8760 CAD per 1 AUD. Multiply.
Step B result = CAD
Step C — Convert CAD back to USD (CAD/USD = 0.7320)
CAD/USD = 0.7320 means 1 CAD = 0.7320 USD:
CAD from Step B × 0.7320 = ? USD
Step C (final USD) = USD
Final — Profit / Loss
Profit = Final USD − Initial $100,000 = ?
Net Profit = USD (approx.)
Hint — full walk-through:
A: $100,000 ÷ 0.6450 ≈ 155,039 AUD
B: 155,039 × 0.8760 ≈ 135,814 CAD
C: 135,814 × 0.7320 ≈ $99,416 USD
Wait — that's a loss! That means the direction is wrong. Try: USD → CAD → AUD → USD instead — sell USD for CAD, buy AUD cheap with CAD at market (0.8760), then sell AUD for USD.
A′: $100,000 ÷ 0.7320 ≈ 136,612 CAD
B′: 136,612 ÷ 0.8760 ≈ 155,949 AUD
C′: 155,949 × 0.6450 ≈ $100,587 USD
Profit ≈ $587 USD on $100K investment.
